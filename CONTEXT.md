# CONTEXT: Automated Droplet Size Measurement (v2, backlit video)

This document explains **what** we are measuring and **why** each design choice was made.
There are two build specs:

- `INSTRUCTIONS_A_frame_extraction.md` covers a **new** notebook that turns recorded videos
  into lossless PNG frames that contain a drop.
- `INSTRUCTIONS_B_measurement_update.md` covers the changes that turn the existing
  `droplet_size_measurement.ipynb` into `droplet_size_measurement_v2.ipynb`.

Read this file first. Don't "optimize away" a decision documented here without checking the
rationale. The original build spec, `INSTRUCTIONS.md`, still describes the v1 notebook (ring-light
stills). v2 keeps its calibration section unchanged. The v1 version of this file is kept as
`CONTEXT_v1_ringlight.md` for history.

## 1. The experiment

- Charged ethanol droplets are generated by an inkjet dispenser and confined in a **Paul trap**.
  The camera images the trap **through the ~1 mm gap between the trap rods**, so the useful
  image is a horizontal **lit band**, roughly rows 215–860 of a 1440×1080 frame. Above and below
  the band the frame is dark.
- **Goal:** measure each drop's diameter (horizontal, vertical and an equivalent diameter) in
  microns, **frame by frame**, so drop shrinkage (evaporation) can be followed in time. The
  size-vs-time plot itself is a **later, separate script**. These two notebooks only have to
  produce a clean per-frame, per-drop CSV with times and drop IDs.

## 2. What changed since v1 (the reason for this rewrite)

| | v1 (Sept 2026) | v2 (Oct 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Illumination | laser + ring light, dark background | **backlight** (shadowgraph). The laser is still on, but the operator will switch it off in future sessions |
| Drop appearance | faint bright ring plus two specular spots | **filled dark disk on a bright background**, with two bright glare spots on its left and right edges while the laser is on |
| Input | hand-saved JPEG stills | **videos** (`.avi`, MJPEG, 1440×1080, 18.005 fps). Frames are extracted by notebook A |
| Magnification | ≈2.43 µm/px | ≈**1.61 µm/px** (new optics, new calibration) |
| Drop size | 14–28 px | **≈17 px when first seen, shrinking to ≈5 px** before it disappears |

The v1 detector (white top-hat, threshold at `bkg + K·σ` on bright pixels, fill holes) assumes
bright drops and **does not apply** to v2 data. The v1 notebook must stay runnable for old data,
so v2 is a **new notebook file**.

## 3. What the v2 data actually looks like (validated on real files, 2026-10-13)

Validation used `2026.10.12/drops/evap_29.avi`, `evap_30.avi` and `evap_31.avi`. All 1 Hz drop
generation, exposure 0.064 ms, 18.005 fps (`r_frame_rate = 3601/200`).

| video | gain / black level | background (band) | noise σ | drop darker by | contrast/noise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| evap_29 | 29.4 dB / 127 | ≈211 | 10.4, rising to ≈21 | ≈150 | ≈14 |
| evap_30 | 16 dB / 0 | ≈46 | 1.5 | ≈35 | ≈23 |
| evap_31 | 16 dB / 0 | ≈46 | 1.5 | ≈35 | ≈23 |

Key properties:

- **The low-gain videos are better** even though they look dark to the eye. Gain amplified
  the noise as much as the signal. **Develop and validate on evap_30 / evap_31.**
  evap_29 is a secondary test only, and only frames **0–450** of it are usable: the camera was
  bumped, so the band drifts ≈20 px downward from ~frame 450 and the noise doubles after ~630.
- **One drop at a time, mostly.** At 1 Hz a drop enters, shrinks smoothly and disappears within
  **≈11–22 frames (≈0.6–1.2 s)**, usually before the next one arrives. It moves while it shrinks:
  fast at first, up to ≈30 px/frame vertically, then a few px/frame. Occasionally two drops are
  in frame or collide. Validation found **34 drop lifetimes of ≥8 frames in evap_30 and 28 in
  evap_31**. The size trajectory repeats closely between drops: equivalent diameter ≈15–17 px →
  ≈5–7 px.
- **Static dark features** (dust on the optics) sit in every frame. evap_30 has one at about
  (x=535, y=708). A per-video **temporal-median background** removes them exactly, and also
  removes the band structure.
- **Glare (laser on):** two bright spots sit **on the left and right rim** of the dark disk and
  often clip at 255. They corrupt horizontal measurements:
  - A row straight through the centre reads **about half** the true width. **Never measure along
    a single central row or column.**
  - Even when edge points inside the glare are excluded, horizontal still reads smaller than
    vertical: **H/V ≈ 0.83–0.86** (median, evap_30 / evap_31). Two possible causes: the glare
    halo darkens the edge less than the true shadow, or the drop is really slightly taller than
    wide. A **laser-off video** will decide this; the operator will record one. Until then, treat
    H as glare-affected and the circle-fit diameter as carrying a few-% systematic uncertainty.
    The circle-fit diameter differed by ≈5% between glare-exclusion rules.
- **Dark-core clipping:** with black level 0 (evap_30/31), the darkest pixels in the middle of
  the drop reach 0. This has only a small effect on a half-depth edge, but it is flagged. Future
  sessions will use a small black-level offset.
- **Compression:** the `.avi` files are MJPEG at about 6–26 Mbit/s. Use the `.avi`, not the `.mp4`,
  which is a lower-bitrate H.264 re-encode. The MJPEG 8×8 blocks are the same scale as the drops,
  which is one reason edge points are fitted over the whole rim (§4c) instead of read from one
  line. Lossless capture is recommended for future sessions.
- **Scale of the problem:** 1 px ≈ 1.6 µm is 6–20% of a drop diameter here. **Sub-pixel edges
  are mandatory.** Frame-to-frame jitter on one drop is about ±1 px. This is acceptable, and the
  later size-vs-time script can smooth it.

## 4. Measurement philosophy (the important decisions)

### 4a. Calibration: unchanged method (grid spacing, least-squares over all lines)
The v1 calibrator (de-rotate, project, least-squares slope of line position vs. index, X and Y
kept separate) **works unchanged on the new slides**. Validated on `2026.10.12/calibration/`:

| image | X px/100 µm (µm/px), RMS | Y px/100 µm (µm/px), RMS |
|---|---|---|
| evap_44.jpg | 62.18 (1.608), 0.31 | 61.48 (1.627), 0.24 |
| evap_46.jpg | 62.17 (1.609), 0.25 | 61.21 (1.634), 0.44 |
| evap_41.jpg | **rejected** (RMS 10 px) | 61.32 (1.631), 0.28 |
| evap_42 / 43 | **rejected** | **rejected** |

- **Median result: X 1.608 µm/px, Y 1.631 µm/px.** The operator's hand value, averaged over 1, 5
  and 10 boxes, was 1.603 µm/px, within 0.3% of X. The spread between the operator's three hand
  ratios (1.587–1.623) is again larger than the automated fit's.
- `evap_45.jpg` is a **hand-rotated export** (1505×1167). It must be excluded, because resampling
  can change the scale.
- `evap_42.jpg` and `evap_43.jpg` are **byte-identical duplicates**. Duplicates must not be
  counted twice in the median.
- The X-axis rejections on evap_41/42/43 are not understood yet (only 2 gridlines found, or
  uneven spacing). Candidates: the long fibre lying across the grid, the printed numbers and
  letters entering the ROI, or the grid only partly inside the band. The rejection logic did its
  job, so this is a robustness item, not a blocker.

### 4b. Background: per-video temporal median, not spatial flattening
Each frame is compared with a background made from the **median of nearby frames of the same
video** (rolling, so a slowly drifting image is tracked). A moving drop occupies any given pixel
in only a few of those frames, so the median is drop-free. The deviation image is
`dev = background − frame`, which is positive where the frame is darker. This one step removes
the band structure, the static dust and the fixed-pattern illumination, and it handles evap_29's
drift. Noise σ is estimated robustly (1.4826 × MAD of `dev` inside the band).

*Limitation (out of scope for now):* a drop that stays **perfectly stationary** for longer than
the median window would leak into the background. That doesn't happen at 1 Hz. It could at
250 Hz, where some drops stay trapped.

### 4c. Edge = half-depth crossing, found on rays and fitted
The drop is now a filled dark disk, so the standard shadowgraph edge applies: **the edge is where
`dev` falls to half the drop's core depth.** The core depth is a robust maximum of `dev` inside
the blob, the 90th percentile. This criterion:

- **is independent of gain and black level**, being a ratio of two measured levels. So videos
  recorded with different gains are directly comparable. This replaces v1's `bkg + K·σ` rule.
- is the right choice now. v1 rejected a half-max rule only because the ring geometry had a
  bright centre dip. A filled disk has no such dip.

Because glare sits on the rim, the edge is found along **72 rays** from the blob centroid (every
5°), each giving a sub-pixel radius by linear interpolation. Then:

1. **Glare rays are excluded:** any ray along which the frame is **brighter** than background by
   more than `GLARE_K·σ` (default 4), anywhere from the centre to a few px past the edge.
2. Rays with no crossing, or radius > 2× the median radius, are dropped. One validation frame
   produced a 130 px "diameter" from a single runaway ray. Then a **robust circle fit** is done
   with one round of residual-outlier rejection.
3. **Reported sizes:**
   - `D_eq`: circle-fit diameter. **Primary size.**
   - `H`, `V`: horizontal and vertical extents of an **ellipse fit** to the same glare-free
     points.
   - `mean_HV = (H+V)/2`, kept for continuity with v1.
   - `aspect = H/V`, flagged when |H−V|/mean > 10%. With the laser on this flag will often trip
     (expected, see §3).

### 4d. Drop identity across frames (`drop_id`)
Size vs time needs to know which detections belong to the same drop. Within each video,
detections in consecutive frames are linked when:

- the frame gap is ≤ 3,
- the centroid moved ≤ 80 px,
- and the diameter did not jump up by > 30% (drops only shrink).

Otherwise a new `drop_id` starts. Validation with these rules produced the lifetimes quoted in
§3. Collisions and two-drop frames can produce wrong links. The QC overlays and a per-drop
trajectory figure are the backstop.

### 4e. QC for every measurement
The visual audit trail stays required. Each saved frame gets a QC overlay showing the drop, the
used and excluded edge points, the fitted circle and ellipse, and the flags. Each `drop_id` also
gets a small "filmstrip" of its frames, so a wrong link or a bad frame is visible at a glance.

## 5. Operating guidance for future sessions (from the operator discussion)
- **Turn the laser off** (backlight only) when measuring size. This removes the glare and should
  make H ≈ V if the drops are round. Record one short laser-on vs laser-off comparison to confirm.
- To make the image brighter, raise the **backlight** first, then exposure (as long as motion
  blur stays ≪ 1 px), and **gain last**. Gain raises noise as much as signal.
- Set a **small black-level offset** so the darkest pixels in the drop's shadow stay above 0
  (about 5–10 counts with the light blocked). Clipping at 0 slightly biases the half-depth edge.
- Prefer **lossless capture** (uncompressed AVI or TIFF/PNG sequence). Drops are 1–2 compression
  blocks across.
- A calibration slide every session, at the same optics. Don't hand-rotate the image file;
  the calibrator de-rotates.

## 6. Non-goals (for these two notebooks)
- The **size-vs-time plot and evaporation-rate fit** belong to a later, separate script.
  These notebooks only produce the CSV it will read (`time_s`, `drop_id`, sizes).
- 250 Hz / 20 Hz / 5 Hz videos (many simultaneous or stationary drops) are not targeted yet.
- No absolute volume or 3-D shape. Only projected diameters.
